📜 Field Notes

Wisdom from the archive.

General patterns in how people actually meet and connect, not predictions about anyone specific.

Network density beats searching

Most people meet partners through a friend of a friend, not a direct search. Each new person you know is a door to several more you don't yet.

Small samples mislead you

Drawing conclusions from one social circle ("there's no one for me") usually just means the sample is too small, not that the pool is empty. Changing the room changes the data.

Geographic spread multiplies options

Being open to people slightly outside your usual commute or usual events meaningfully increases who you'll ever cross paths with.

Consistency predicts more than chemistry

Early spark fades or grows either way. Whether someone follows through on plans and communicates clearly is a much stronger long-term signal than how strong the first impression was.

Ambiguity has a cost

Time spent in an undefined situation is time not spent meeting people who are actually ready to be direct with you. It's fine to enjoy someone's company, just don't let "maybe" quietly become a placeholder for years.

Shared values outlast shared aesthetics

Attraction can grow toward someone whose character and life direction fit yours, even if they weren't your instinctive type on sight.

Hosting is a force multiplier

Organizing a gathering, even a small one, tends to introduce you to more new, relevant people per hour than attending one does.
